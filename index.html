Observational Geometry & Selection Effects

The distribution of cataloged exoplanets is fundamentally governed by observational detection thresholds. Space observatories such as Kepler and TESS account for the majority of discoveries via the Transit method (4,785 records), which detects periodic photometric stellar dips, while ground spectrographs contribute via Radial Velocity (1,204 records) measuring Doppler reflex motion.

Scientific Caution: Habitability Interpretation

Equilibrium temperature ($T_{eq}$) represents a theoretical blackbody value. It does not establish surface habitability: atmospheric composition, volatile inventory, surface liquid water, magnetospheric shielding, and stellar flare activity are critical determinants.

Methodological Limitations & Observational Bias

  • Archive Evolution: The NASA Exoplanet Archive continuously updates with revised measurements as spectroscopic and transit timing follow-ups occur.
  • Selection Bias: Transiting surveys (Kepler, TESS) preferentially detect planets with short orbital periods and larger radii orbiting close to their parent stars.
  • Composite Parameters: Values in `pscomppars` represent composite parameters compiled across multiple peer-reviewed publications and may not represent a single self-consistent orbital solution.
  • Correlation vs. Causation: Statistical associations reflect both planet formation physics and observational detectability cutoffs.
